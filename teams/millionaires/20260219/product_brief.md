# Product Brief — QuestCity

**Team Name:** Team Millionaires (DG-ST-AB)
**Date:** February 19, 2026

---

## The Problem

People living in cities feel stuck doing the same things every weekend. It's not that there's nothing to do — it's that finding something new feels effortful, uncertain, and never quite worth it. The typical Saturday evening ends with everyone defaulting to the same bar.

**Who has this problem?**

Couples and friend groups aged 25–35 in urban areas who have discretionary time and income but consistently under-explore their own city. Also solo explorers who want a reason to go out but lack structure.

**How do they currently deal with it?**

They open Yelp or Google Maps, scroll for 20 minutes, get overwhelmed by options, and give up. Or they make plans to "check out something new" and never follow through because nothing specific enough to act on comes up.

**How painful is it?**

From interviews: multiple people described a specific guilt — *"I've lived here for three years and I still haven't been to half the neighborhoods."* Two interviewees described cancelling plans because they couldn't agree on what to do. Pain score averaged 7–8/10 across 8 interviews.

Key interview phrases:
- "We just go to the same three places."
- "I keep saving restaurants on Instagram and never going."
- "I want to explore more but it never feels urgent enough."

---

## The Solution

QuestCity is a gamified outdoor exploration app that turns your neighborhood into an adventure game. You pick a quest ("Coffee Connoisseur: Visit 5 indie coffee shops in your area"), check in at each location using a photo, earn points and badges, and unlock new quests as you progress.

**What does it do?**

It gives people a specific reason to go somewhere they've never been before, structured as a game with clear goals, progress, and rewards.

**How does the user interact with it?**

Open the app → browse a quest board → pick a quest → get sent to your first location → check in with a photo → earn points → complete the quest → get a badge and unlock the next tier.

**What makes it different?**

Google Maps tells you *where* things are. QuestCity tells you *why to go there and what to do when you get there.* The game layer removes decision fatigue and replaces it with a clear mission.

---

## Target User

**Demographics:**
- 25–35 years old
- Lives in a major city (NYC launch)
- Has free weekends and $20–50/week in discretionary spending
- Socially active, active on Instagram

**Defining behavior:**
- Has saved dozens of places on Google Maps and never visited most of them
- Often plans to "do something new this weekend" but defaults to familiar spots
- Would rather have a structured plan than browse infinitely

**How they describe the problem:**
- "I feel like I'm not taking advantage of living here."
- "We always end up at the same places."
- "I need a reason to go somewhere, not just a list."

---

## Why Now

The gamification layer (Duolingo, Strava, Pokémon GO) has trained people to expect game mechanics in behavior-change products. Applying this to local discovery hasn't been done well. The tools exist (Google Places API, photo check-in, points systems) and AI makes quest generation and personalization cheap to build.

---

## Key Interview Evidence

- **Strongest quote:** *"I feel like I'm wasting my city."* — Interviewee #4, 28, lives in Manhattan
- **Pattern:** All 8 interviewees reported defaulting to the same 3–5 places; all wanted to explore more but cited effort/motivation as the blocker
- **Surprise:** Solo explorers were as interested as couples — we'd assumed this was primarily a couple problem
- **Current spend/effort:** People were already paying for Yelp Plus, Google Maps Premium, and Eventbrite tickets — they're willing to pay to solve this
