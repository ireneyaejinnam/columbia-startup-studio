# **Demand Test Plan — Tripable**

## 

## **Audience**

Our demand test will target college students and recent graduates who are planning group trips with friends.

This audience is specific because they often travel in groups, coordinate through group chats, and deal with the exact planning problems Tripable is solving: deciding where to go, finding dates that work for everyone, organizing links and ideas, and keeping track of expenses.

**Target profile**

* Ages 18–26  
* College students, grad students, or recent graduates  
* Interested in travel, weekend trips, spring break trips, graduation trips, or friend-group vacations  
* Located in major college cities or urban areas  
* Likely using Instagram, TikTok, group chats, Google Docs, Splitwise, and Doodle/When2Meet to plan trips

## **Angles**

We will test two different message angles to see which problem resonates more strongly.

**Angle A: “Group chat chaos”**  
This angle focuses on the pain of planning trips across messy group chats.

**Message:**  
“Planning a trip with friends shouldn’t mean losing dates, links, budgets, and ideas in the group chat. Tripable keeps everything in one shared space so your group can actually make decisions.”

**Why we are testing it:**  
This angle targets the emotional frustration of group planning. It is relatable and easy for people to understand quickly because most friend groups already use group chats to plan.

**Angle B: “Make decisions faster”**  
This angle focuses on Tripable as a decision-making tool for groups.

**Message:**  
“Stop spending weeks asking ‘where should we go?’ and ‘when is everyone free?’ Tripable helps your group vote on destinations, compare availability, build an itinerary, and split expenses in one place.”

**Why we are testing it:**  
This angle is more functional. It highlights what the product actually helps users do and may attract people who are actively planning a trip right now.

## **Budget**

We will run a small paid demand test with a total budget of approximately $200.

Budget breakdown:

* Total budget: $200  
* Daily spend: \~$40/day  
* Test length: 5 days  
* Split: $100 for Angle A and $100 for Angle B

Each angle will be tested with the same target audience and similar visual style so the main difference is the message, not the design.

## **Success Threshold**

Before interpreting results, we will commit to the following success threshold:

Primary success metric: email signups or waitlist signups.

We will consider the test successful if either:

* We get at least 40 total signups across both ads, or  
* One angle achieves at least a 10% landing page conversion rate from clicks to signups.

We will also compare the two angles against each other. The stronger angle will be the one with the higher signup conversion rate, not just the higher click-through rate.

If neither angle reaches the success threshold, we will interpret that as a signal that either the message is not strong enough, the audience is too broad, or the landing page does not make the value clear enough.
