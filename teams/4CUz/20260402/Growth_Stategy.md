   2. Offer: Early access; ability to influence features  
   3. Target volume: 5–10 groups, 1–2 posts per group  
3. Columbia students  
   1. Say: “Hey, quick question, have you ever planned a trip with friends and it turned into a mess in group chats? I’m building something called Tripable that organizes everything from voting, itinerary, splitting costs, etc., all in one place. I’m looking for a few Columbia students to try it for their next trip.”  
   2. Offer: Early access; direct input on features; possibly small incentive (raffle, coffee gift card, etc.)  
   3. Target volume: 10–20 conversations

**What success looks like**

* If our user count increases/more signups  
* There’s a lot of engagement/reactions to posts/comments in the thread/community  
* People are actually willing to use it and share with their friends/family  
* Qualitative feedback (pain points, feature requests)

**What Worked**

The best-performing channels were Discord / student group chats and Columbia student outreach.

Discord and student group chats gave us the highest number of clicks and signups. This makes sense because people in those groups already fit our target audience: students who plan activities and trips with friends. The message also felt natural in that environment because it was framed as a student project looking for testers, not as a product ad.

Columbia student outreach produced fewer clicks overall, but it gave us stronger feedback. When we showed people the product in person, they were more willing to explain where they got confused. A few users immediately understood the problem because they had recently planned trips using group chats, Google Docs, or Splitwise.

The strongest signal was that users understood the pain point quickly. Most people agreed that group trip planning gets messy when decisions are spread across too many places.

**What Did Not Work**

Reddit was less effective than expected. People were willing to discuss the problem, but fewer people clicked through and signed up. The post worked better as a discovery channel than as an acquisition channel.

The biggest drop-off happened after signup. Several users created an account but did not immediately create a trip. This suggests that the landing page and concept were clear enough to get some interest, but the first in-product action was not obvious enough.

The main onboarding issue was that users were unsure what to do first after entering the product. Some expected to invite friends first, while others expected to add a destination or dates. This means the dashboard needs a stronger guided flow for first-time users.

**How We Interpret the Results**

Our data suggests that Tripable’s problem resonates with the target audience, but the product needs a clearer activation path.

The strongest channels were the ones with a student context and higher trust. Discord, student group chats, and in-person Columbia outreach worked better than Reddit because users were closer to our target audience and more willing to try a student-built product.

The main issue is not that people do not understand the problem. The issue is that once they enter the product, they need clearer guidance on the first step.

**What We Are Changing Next**

Based on these results, we are changing the onboarding flow so that new users are guided through one clear path:

Create a trip  
Add basic trip details  
Invite friends or copy an invite link  
Choose the first planning action

We will also improve the empty states inside the trip dashboard. Instead of showing blank sections, each section should explain what it is for and give the user one clear next step.

Examples:

“Add your first date option”  
“Invite friends to start planning together”  
“Add the first itinerary item”  
“Track the first shared expense”

**Next Growth Decision**

We are going to double down on student communities and in-person Columbia outreach.

Reddit was useful for general feedback, but it did not produce enough activated users. Discord and Columbia outreach produced better users because they were closer to our target audience and more likely to understand the use case.

For the next round, we will focus on getting small groups of connected students to try Tripable together. This matters because Tripable is more useful when people use it with their actual friend group, not just as individual testers. This follows our original channel plan around Reddit, Discord/student group chats, and Columbia student outreach.

**Part 3 \- Cold Start**

**1\. What is your cold start type?**

Our cold start type is Group / Invite.

Tripable only becomes useful when more than one person is planning together. One user can create a trip, but the real value comes when friends join, add availability, vote on ideas, comment, and help build the plan. If someone uses Tripable alone, it feels more like a personal notes app. If a whole friend group joins, it becomes the shared planning space we are trying to build.

**2\. Who is your hard side?**

Our hard side is the first friend group that actually has an upcoming trip and is willing to plan inside Tripable together.

More specifically, our hard side is:  
A group of 4–8 Columbia students who are already planning a summer, weekend, or post-finals trip and are currently using a group chat to coordinate.

Examples of specific people we can target:

* The Columbia students in our own extended friend groups who are planning summer travel  
* A friend group planning a weekend trip after finals  
* A student club group planning a retreat or end-of-semester trip  
* A group of seniors planning a graduation trip  
* A group of students interning in the same city this summer and trying to plan weekend activities

The key is not just “college students.” We need a group that already knows each other, already has a reason to plan together, and already feels the pain of messy coordination.

**3\. What are your two strategies?**

**Primary Strategy: Network Density**

Our primary strategy is to get one small, connected group to use Tripable together instead of trying to get many random individual users.

Tripable needs density because the product is more valuable when the people in the same trip all join. A small group of 5 friends using Tripable for one real trip is more useful than 50 disconnected signups who never invite anyone.

**Secondary Strategy: Manual / Concierge Onboarding**

Our secondary strategy is to manually help the first groups set up their trips.

Instead of expecting users to figure everything out alone, we can help them create the trip, add sample sections, invite friends, and show them how to use availability, itinerary, and expenses. This reduces friction and helps us learn where people get stuck.
