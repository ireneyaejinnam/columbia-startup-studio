# **Tripable User Testing \+ Growth Plan**

## **Track A: Qualitative — User Testing**

## **User Testing Plan**

### **Product**

Tripable is a shared trip-planning workspace for friend groups. Instead of planning across scattered group chats, Google Docs, calendar screenshots, travel links, and payment apps, Tripable gives the group one place to coordinate the trip.

### **Goal of the Test**

We are testing whether new users understand Tripable’s onboarding flow without explanation.

The main question is:

**Can a first-time user understand what Tripable is, sign up, and reach the first useful planning moment without help?**

For Tripable, the activation moment is when a user creates or joins a trip and realizes the group can coordinate dates, destinations, itinerary items, and expenses in one shared space.

---

## **Who We Will Recruit**

We will recruit **3–5 people** who fit our target user profile but do not know our product.

### **Target Users**

Our target users are college students, graduate students, or recent graduates who have planned or joined a group trip with friends before. These users are likely already using group chats, Google Docs, Doodle, When2Meet, Splitwise, or similar tools to coordinate plans.

### **Recruitment Channels**

We will recruit from:

Columbia students we do not personally know  
 Instagram or LinkedIn cold DMs to students and recent graduates  
 Reddit communities related to college, travel, or paid user testing  
 Discord or Slack communities for students and young professionals

### **Compensation**

For strangers, we will offer **$10 for a 20-minute session** through Venmo or PayPal.

---

## **Consent**

For strangers recruited through Reddit, Craigslist, cold DM, or online communities, we will use a short written consent form before the session.

The consent form will explain that the session is for testing a student-built product, notes may be used for class and product improvement, no sensitive personal information will be shared, the participant may stop at any time, and screen recording will only happen with permission.

For classmates or people we know well, verbal consent is acceptable.

---

## **Session Script**

### **Intro**

“Thanks for helping us test Tripable. I’m going to have you try to use our product. Please think out loud as you go — say what you’re looking at, what you expect to happen, and anything that feels confusing. I won’t help you during the test because we’re testing the product, not you. There are no wrong answers.”

### **Task 1: First Impression**

“Go to the Tripable website. Before signing up, tell me what you think this product does.”

We are watching whether users understand that Tripable is for group trip planning, whether they understand who it is for, and whether they know what action to take next.

### **Task 2: Sign Up / Sign In Flow**

“Please try to create an account or enter the product.”

We are watching whether users find the right call-to-action, understand whether they should sign in or sign up, hesitate on the auth page, or complete the flow without confusion.

### **Task 3: Create or Join a Trip**

“Imagine you and three friends are planning a weekend trip. Try to start planning that trip in Tripable.”

We are watching whether users can create a trip, understand what information is required, understand what the trip workspace is, and know what to do after the trip is created.

### **Task 4: Reach the Aha Moment**

“Try to figure out what the group should do next after creating the trip.”

We are watching whether users discover the main planning sections, understand availability, itinerary, voting, or expenses, and understand why Tripable is useful compared to a group chat.

---

## **Post-Session Questions**

When was the first moment you understood what Tripable is for?

What was the most confusing part?

What did you expect to happen that did not happen?

Would you use this for a real group trip? Why or why not?

If you could change one thing, what would it be?

---

## **What We Will Record**

We will focus on behavior, not just opinions.

We will record where users pause, where users click the wrong thing, what language confuses them, whether they reach the trip workspace, whether they understand the next step after signing up, and whether they can explain the product back in their own words.

---

## **User Testing Synthesis**

### **What we observed**

Across our sessions, we observed that users understood the general idea of group trip planning, but they needed the onboarding flow to make the next step more obvious. The biggest point of friction was not the concept of Tripable itself; it was knowing what to do immediately after entering the product.

Users expected the product to guide them toward one clear first action, such as creating a trip, inviting friends, or adding availability. When the page showed multiple planning options at once, users hesitated and were unsure which step mattered most.

### **What we are changing**

We are changing the onboarding flow so that the first-time user is guided through one clear path:

Create a trip  
 Add basic trip details  
 Invite friends or copy a share link  
 Add availability or choose the first planning section

We will also add clearer empty states inside the trip dashboard. Instead of showing blank sections, each empty section will explain what it is for and include a single call-to-action, such as “Add dates,” “Invite friends,” or “Add first itinerary item.”

### **Why**

The sessions showed that users understand the pain of group trip planning, but they need a more directed first experience. Tripable’s value becomes clear once the user sees a shared trip workspace. The faster we get users to that workspace and show them one useful action, the more likely they are to activate.

This change should improve activation because it reduces the number of decisions a new user has to make before experiencing the product’s value.
