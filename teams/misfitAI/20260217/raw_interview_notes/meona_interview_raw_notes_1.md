## Context

- Interviewee is a cybersecurity engineer from the Bay Area.
- Mostly works from home.
- Found through a social event.
- Usually dresses simply.
- Rotates between the same pants, jeans, sweaters, and jackets.
- Often wears a simple black t-shirt with a sweater or sweatshirt over it.

## Wardrobe and Outfit Behavior

- Did not bring up the wardrobe-management problem unprompted.
- Tends to gravitate toward certain pieces of clothing over others.
- Dressing depends heavily on energy level that morning.
- Some statement pieces go unworn for weeks.
- Outfit choice is not just about indecision; it is more based on how he feels that day.
- He asks himself, "Which would I feel most confident in that day?"
- Sometimes texts people in the morning for outfit opinions.

## Fit and Shopping Pain Points

- Size is the most annoying clothing-related issue for him to keep track of.
- Finds sizing inconsistent across brands.
- A medium in one store may not fit like a medium in another store.
- Prefers in-person shopping because he can try things on.
- Finding clothes for his specific body is more important than discovering new clothes or outfit pairings.
- He has not spent money on a tool to solve this problem.

## Fashion Discovery

- Does not look to social media for fashion trends.
- Thinks social media is too trend-forward.
- Gets ideas from seeing people out and about on the street.

## Reaction to Digital Wardrobe Assistant

- Initial reaction was that the idea was cool.
- Believes he may not be the target demographic because he likes to do outfit decisions himself.
- Would not want the process to be fully automated.
- Likes the idea of a digital wardrobe inventory.
- Forgets what is in his wardrobe while shopping.
- Wants to know whether an item matches clothes he already owns, such as whether one black item is the same black as another or whether an item is more formal.
- Seeing clothes on his own body would be helpful.
- Statistics on how many times he has worn something recently could be useful.

## Commitments and Signal

- Would not pre-purchase or pay because he expects infrequent use.
- Might download and try it if it were free.
- Even a 99-cent price creates a barrier to entry for him.
- Open to follow-up if features align better with his needs and connect with stores he shops at.
- Offered referrals to dads in his home community because he thought they might like the product.
- Overall signal was mixed/yellow: some pain around fit and inventory, but low urgency and low willingness to pay.
