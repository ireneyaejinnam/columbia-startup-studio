# Weekly Update — August Fan Club (Evidence of Life)
**Week of April 13, 2026**

## What we built last week and why

We shipped two of the three sign-up fixes: the sign-in/sign-up form now has a clear tab toggle with a "Confirm password" field in sign-up mode, and the email confirmation link redirects to the login page with a success message instead of the blank "no session" screen. The in-app browser detection for Google OAuth is in progress. We prioritized these because our user testing showed 5/5 participants hitting friction in the first 30 seconds — and we couldn't grow the user base until that was resolved.

We also onboarded our first concierge users. Early signal: users understand the daily timeline view quickly, but the "add places" flow remains the biggest usability gap. Multiple users tried to add a place and gave up. This confirms what we heard in the roadmap review — Places is the feature people care about most, and it's also the one that's hardest to use.

## What we're building this week and why

Redesigning the "add places" UX — this is the #1 usability blocker from both user testing and concierge feedback. We're also adding competitive positioning copy to the landing page. The positioning question has sharpened: users keep benchmarking us against Notion, Apple Notes, even Instagram and paper journals. We need to make the distinction visible — Evidence of Life connects moments, AI tags, map, calendar, and reflection in one place. None of the alternatives touch more than one or two of those. Eric is launching the email nurture sequence to the waitlist to convert signups into active users now that the sign-up flow works.

## Current user count

Registered users: ~18. Second wave of signups came from organic social posts (the "plans vs. reality" narrative angle landed well) and continued word of mouth at Columbia. User interviews conducted to date: ~25 and growing.

## Biggest blocker

The "add places" UX. It's blocking the feature users care about most. If we can't make adding a place intuitive, we lose the strongest hook in the product. Willow is sequencing the places UX redesign before anything else this week.
