# Startup Funnel Report
 
## 1. Funnel
 
We onboarded friends and family through manual, one-on-one outreach. Because each conversation was personal and direct, our conversion rate has been very high — over 90% of people we reached out to signed up. This brought us to **30 registered users** in total.
 
## 2. Analysis
 
**Biggest drop-off: retention after first use.** Of our 30 registered users, only **1 is currently active**. The drop-off occurs *after* users have fully tried the app, which suggests the issue isn't onboarding friction or first-impression confusion — it's that the product isn't yet delivering enough sustained value to justify continued use.
 
**Why this is happening.** Our app introduces a productivity and tracking habit that is meaningfully different from what most people are already used to. Switching costs in this category are unusually high: people have existing systems (notes apps, calendars, task managers, or simply nothing at all), and asking them to genuinely adopt a new daily habit is a steep ask. Most testers found it hard to make the switch stick, even when they liked the concept.
 
**What we're doing about it.** We're actively collecting user feedback and iterating based on what we hear. However, a core challenge has emerged: **everyone has a different idea of what they want from a productivity / life-management app.** Despite multiple rounds of iteration, the current product still doesn't feel compelling to the broad mix of users we've invited. Feedback pulls in different directions, and trying to satisfy everyone risks building something that resonates with no one.
 
**Takeaway.** The bottleneck is no longer acquisition — it's product-market fit within a narrower user segment. Our next step is to identify which subset of our testers most resembles our ideal long-term user, and prioritize iteration around their needs specifically rather than averaging feedback across the whole group.
